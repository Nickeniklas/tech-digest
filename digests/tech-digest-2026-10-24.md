## Google may use your Gmail and Photos to target ads

**What happened**
The Verge reports that Google has updated its privacy policy, tied to its 'personal intelligence' AI features, to say it might use data from your Gmail and Google Photos to target ads.

**What this means**
If you use a personal Google account, your emails and photos could shape the ads you see. It's worth checking your Google privacy settings — and if you're a lawyer, manager or anyone who handles client material, a reminder to keep work out of personal accounts.

Source: [The Verge ↗](https://www.theverge.com/tech/1017600/google-personal-intelligence-privacy-policy-ads-update)

## Quick Hits

### Anthropic maps out possible futures for an AI economy

Anthropic published 'Scenarios for our economic future', which starts from an honest admission: nobody yet knows how AI will reshape the economy. It lays out the possibilities — unprecedented growth, widespread unemployment, neither, or something else — and asks how we'll tell which one is happening. Useful framing if you're planning a career or a team for the next few years.

Source: [Anthropic ↗](https://www.anthropic.com/institute/econ-scenarios)

### From a health worker's notebook to an Ebola situation report

Anthropic's feature 'The Situation Report' follows the Ebola response in the eastern Democratic Republic of the Congo, where community health workers record fevers, recoveries and deaths and text their notes over WhatsApp. Those counts become the situation reports responders rely on — a concrete look at how messy, real-world information gets turned into decisions.

Source: [Anthropic ↗](https://www.anthropic.com/features/ebola-response)

### Kimi releases K3.1 Pro and a faster Highspeed version

Kimi, the AI assistant from Chinese company Moonshot AI, released two new models: K3.1 Pro and K3.1 Pro Highspeed. The pairing — one model for quality, one for speed — mirrors how most AI companies now let you trade a little accuracy for quicker answers.

Source: [Kimi ↗](https://www.kimi.ai/blog/kimi-k3-1-pro)

### LitPivot helps researchers see where their idea fits

The Allen Institute for AI introduced LitPivot, a tool that helps researchers develop ideas by showing them how relevant papers change the picture. A new paper might reveal someone already did your idea, or point to an open problem worth chasing — LitPivot is built around those moments.

Source: [Hugging Face Blog ↗](https://huggingface.co/blog/allenai/litpivot)

## Under the Hood

### Hugging Face lists which models run on AWS Trainium

**What happened**
Previously covered on 2026-10-20: Hugging Face introduced TorchNeuron, a native PyTorch backend for Amazon's Trainium AI chips. Since then, it has published a way to look up which Transformers models on the Hub actually run on Trainium, since in principle any of them may.

**Why it matters**
For teams choosing cloud hardware, 'it might work' isn't enough. A lookup list turns Trainium from an experiment into something you can plan around — and gives Amazon's chips a clearer case against Nvidia GPUs on cost.

Source: [Hugging Face Blog ↗](https://huggingface.co/blog/torch-neuronx-model-support)

### Astral brings its uv and ty Python tools together

**What happened**
Astral, the company behind uv (a fast tool for installing Python packages and managing projects) and ty (a tool that checks Python code for type errors before it runs), announced an integration between the two.

**Why it matters**
Python developers often juggle several separate tools to set up, run and check a project. Combining them into one workflow means less configuration and fewer places for things to break — a big part of why uv has spread so quickly.

Source: [Astral ↗](https://astral.sh/blog/uv-ty-integration)
