## AI Reads an Entire Ancient Scroll Burned by Vesuvius

**What happened**
Researchers say they have read a complete carbonized scroll from Herculaneum — buried by the eruption of Mount Vesuvius in 79 AD — for the first time, without physically opening it. They used machine learning to virtually unwrap high-resolution CT scans and pick out the faint traces of ink on the brittle, rolled-up papyrus.

**What this means**
A library of texts long thought unreadable is now within reach, which could expand what we know about the ancient world. For teachers, historians, and researchers, it is a clear example of AI used for discovery and preservation rather than automation.

Source: [Vesuvius Challenge ↗](https://scrollprize.org/firstscroll)

### IBM Unveils Chip Technology Smaller Than a Nanometer

IBM says it has demonstrated the world's first sub-1-nanometer chip technology, pushing past a size barrier the industry has been approaching for years. Smaller features generally mean more computing power packed into the same space, which matters for the energy-hungry AI systems that increasingly run everything from search to office software.

Source: [IBM Newsroom ↗](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-06-25-ibm-debuts-worlds-first-sub-1-nanometer-chip-technology)

### Google Releases a 'DESIGN.md' Standard for AI Coding Agents

Google Labs published design.md, a simple format for describing a brand's visual identity — colors, fonts, spacing, tone — in a single file that AI coding assistants can read. The goal is to give agents a consistent understanding of a design system so the interfaces they build actually look on-brand. For designers and marketers, it is an attempt to keep AI-generated work inside the lines of an existing style.

Source: [GitHub (Google Labs) ↗](https://github.com/google-labs-code/design.md)

### A Free Tool Turns Messy PDFs Into AI-Ready Text

MinerU, an open-source project trending on GitHub, converts complicated documents like PDFs and Office files into clean, structured text that AI tools can actually work with. For anyone who wrangles contracts, reports, or research papers, it removes a common first hurdle: getting a messy document into a form an AI assistant can read reliably.

## Under the Hood

### GitHub Benchmarks Its Copilot 'Agentic Harness' Across 20+ Models

**What happened**
GitHub published an evaluation of its Copilot agentic harness — the system that lets the AI plan and carry out multi-step coding tasks rather than just suggest a line at a time. It measured both the quality of results and how efficiently each model used tokens (the units of text an AI processes and bills for) across more than 20 different models.

**Why it matters**
The takeaway is that the harness around a model — how it gathers context, picks tools, and routes work — is becoming as important as the model itself. Better token efficiency means lower cost for the same result, which matters now that Copilot bills by usage rather than a flat fee.

Source: [GitHub Blog ↗](https://github.blog/ai-and-ml/github-copilot/evaluating-performance-and-efficiency-of-the-github-copilot-agentic-harness-across-models-and-tasks/)

### What Happened When 2,000 People Tried to Hack One AI Assistant

**What happened**
A developer set up a public challenge inviting people to break their AI assistant — tricking it into ignoring its instructions or leaking information it shouldn't. Roughly 2,000 attempts later, they wrote up which attacks worked, which failed, and what actually held the system together.

**Why it matters**
These 'prompt injection' attacks are the central security problem for any AI assistant connected to real data or tools. Seeing thousands of real-world attempts in one place is a useful, honest look at how fragile — or sturdy — these systems are once strangers start probing them.

Source: [fernandoi.cl ↗](https://www.fernandoi.cl/posts/hackmyclaw/)
