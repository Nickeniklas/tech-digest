## Claude Haiku 5.5: Anthropic's cheapest, fastest small model

**What happened**
Anthropic released Claude Haiku 5.5, which it calls its fastest, cheapest and most capable small model yet. It is built for high-volume work where cost per request matters.

**What this means**
Small models like Haiku power many of the AI features inside the apps you already use — summaries, email drafts, support chat. A cheaper, faster version usually means those features get quicker and more widely available, and it lowers the cost for teams building their own AI tools.

Source: [Anthropic ↗](https://www.anthropic.com/claude-haiku-5-5)

SynthID Detector, a tool for checking content for SynthID — Google's invisible watermark on AI-generated media — now has its own public site. If you need to judge whether an image or text came from an AI tool, this is one more way to check.

Source: [SynthID ↗](https://synthid.com/)

## Under the Hood

### Liquid AI releases open d1 decision models for devices

**What happened**
Liquid AI released two open "decision models" — small models built to pick the right action rather than write long answers: d1-3B and an experimental d1-omni-600M that also handles more than text. They are designed to run on the device itself, not in the cloud.

**Why it matters**
Decision models keep arriving this month, and running them locally means faster responses and data that never leaves the device — useful for agents on phones, laptops and embedded hardware.

Source: [Hugging Face Blog (Liquid AI) ↗](https://huggingface.co/blog/LiquidAI/open-d1)

### NVIDIA fine-tunes Nemotron to gold-level olympiad results

**What happened**
NVIDIA describes how it fine-tuned one open model family, Nemotron, to reach gold-level results at both the International Olympiad in Informatics (programming) and the International Mathematical Olympiad. The recipe teaches the model to prove, check and revise its work, combined with extra thinking time when answering.

**Why it matters**
The models, data and recipes are published openly on Hugging Face, so others can reuse the same specialisation approach rather than starting from scratch.

Source: [Hugging Face Blog (NVIDIA) ↗](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/nemotron-ioi-and-imo-2026)
