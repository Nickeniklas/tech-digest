## Your AI Assistant Can Now Remember Yesterday's Work

**What happened**
A developer published claude-mem, a free tool that captures everything an AI agent does during a session, compresses it, and automatically injects the relevant context the next time you open a conversation. It works with Claude Code, GitHub Copilot, Codex, Gemini, and more.

**What this means**
Most AI assistants start fresh every session — you have to re-explain your project, your preferences, and where you left off. With persistent memory like this, a teacher could return to a half-built lesson plan, a marketer could pick up a campaign brief, or a lawyer could continue drafting a document without re-briefing the tool from scratch.

Source: [GitHub ↗](https://github.com/thedotmack/claude-mem)

## Under the Hood

### A Japanese Tech Firm Open-Sourced a Physics Solver for 3D Simulations

**What happened**
ST Technology released ppf-contact-solver, an open-source library for simulating how 3D objects physically interact — cloth folding without clipping, solids compressing and bouncing, ropes bending and tangling. This kind of code normally lives inside expensive proprietary game engines or university research software.

**Why it matters**
Accurate physics simulation has historically required either costly licensed software or years of specialization to build. An open-source, well-engineered contact solver lowers the barrier for developers building games, visual effects tools, robotics environments, and medical training simulations. ST Technology is a firm with a reputation for rigorous engineering — an open license from them carries weight.

Source: [GitHub ↗](https://github.com/st-tech/ppf-contact-solver)
