Lead Story

AI prices have fallen by up to 80% in a few months

Data scientist Max Woolf looks at why OpenAI and Anthropic have cut the prices of their AI models by up to 80% within just a few months. Anthropic's newest model, Claude Opus 5.5, is one example: the company says it costs 40% less to run than its predecessor on typical workloads.

What else happened today

▸ A decade-old specialist AI beats today's chatbots at spotting skin cancer Lynch Lab

Researchers report that a 10-year-old image-recognition model built only for skin cancer diagnosis outperforms today's leading general-purpose AI models at the task. It's a useful reminder that the most famous AI isn't automatically the best tool for a specific, high-stakes job — especially in healthcare.

VIA Lynch Lab ↗

▸ IBM's CEO: open AI models are now good and cheap enough to run in-house The Register

IBM's chief executive says open-weight models — AI models that companies can download and run on their own computers — are now 'irresistibly good and cheap' to run on-premises, The Register reports. For organisations that handle sensitive data, such as law firms or hospitals, that makes keeping AI inside their own walls a more realistic option.

VIA The Register ↗

▸ DreamHost is forcing password resets during an active incident DreamHost Status

Web hosting company DreamHost's status page reports an active incident that is forcing customers to reset their passwords. If your website, blog or email is hosted with DreamHost, check your account and the status page now.

VIA DreamHost Status ↗

▸ Losing a job to AI costs more than a paycheck The Economist

The Economist argues that if AI displaces human workers, they will lose more than just their pay. It's a prompt to think about what work gives people beyond income — something managers planning AI rollouts may want to keep in mind.

VIA The Economist ↗

Reflection releases Beam, a 501-billion-parameter open model

Hugging Face welcomed Beam, a new open model with 501 billion parameters — the internal settings a model learns during training, and a rough measure of its size. It's pitched as a model you can own, customise and scale yourself rather than renting access through someone else's service.

ModelSizePitch
Beam501 billion parametersOpen model you can own, customise and scale
Why it matters

Large open models like this are what make the 'run it in-house' argument from IBM's CEO practical. For engineering teams, a model you control means you can fine-tune it on your own data and keep it on your own infrastructure.

Source Hugging Face Blog ↗
Deep Dive

The state of context engineering

Writer Drew Breunig surveys the state of context engineering — the practice of carefully choosing what information you feed an AI model alongside your request, so it has exactly what it needs to do the job well.

Why it matters

As models get cheaper, much of the difference between a good and a bad AI tool comes down to what context it's given. This is for anyone building AI features or agents who wants a current overview of the techniques.
