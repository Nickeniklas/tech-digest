## AI makes law firms faster — clients want a discount

**What happened**
The New York Times reports that as AI tools speed up legal work, clients are pushing their law firms for lower fees. The pressure lands on the billable hour, the long-standing practice of charging for time spent rather than for results.

**What this means**
If you buy professional services — legal, consulting, agency work — it's now reasonable to ask how AI has changed the hours on your invoice. If you sell them, expect that question, and think about pricing by outcome rather than by time.

## Under the Hood

### A model that doesn't train beat tuned XGBoost 14 times out of 14

**What happened**
A new benchmark write-up compares TabPFN and TabICL — pre-trained models that make predictions on a spreadsheet-style dataset without being trained on it — against XGBoost, the long-time standard for tabular data, carefully tuned. The no-training approach won on all 14 comparisons.

**Why it matters**
Most business data lives in tables, and XGBoost has been the default tool for it for a decade. If pre-trained tabular models hold up, analysts could get strong predictions without the slow tuning step. Worth testing on your own data before switching.

Source: [efraingaray.com ↗](https://efraingaray.com/en/blog/tabpfn-vs-xgboost/)

### GitHub made its site faster by shipping more CSS

**What happened**
GitHub fully migrated github.com away from CSS-in-JS — a technique where page styling is generated by JavaScript as the page runs — back to plain stylesheets.

**Why it matters**
It's a counter-intuitive result: sending more CSS up front turned out faster than computing styles in the browser. For teams building web apps, it's a large-scale case study that the older, simpler approach can win on performance.

Source: [GitHub Blog ↗](https://github.blog/engineering/architecture-optimization/improving-site-performance-by-shipping-more-css/)
