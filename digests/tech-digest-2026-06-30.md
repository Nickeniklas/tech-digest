## South Korea Bets $1 Trillion on Chips and Robots

**What happened**
South Korea unveiled a roughly $1 trillion plan to expand memory-chip production and build out a homegrown humanoid-robot industry — a national wager on the physical hardware that the AI boom depends on.

**What this means**
Memory chips are the raw material of every AI data center, so an investment this size shapes chip supply, prices, and how fast the robotics wave reaches real workplaces. For anyone planning around AI — operations leads, manufacturers, investors — it's a clear signal of where the next decade of capacity is being poured.

## Quick Hits

### Supreme Court Reins In 'Geofence' Location Warrants

The US Supreme Court ruled that geofence warrants — which let police demand data on every phone near a crime scene — need constitutional protections. The decision curbs a fast-growing surveillance technique that routinely sweeps up bystanders along with suspects, and it gives lawyers a fresh line of defense in cases built on location data.

Source: [The Guardian ↗](https://www.theguardian.com/us-news/2026/jun/29/supreme-court-geofence-warrants-case-decision)

### GitHub Copilot Switches to Pay-Per-Use Pricing

GitHub is moving its Copilot coding assistant to usage-based billing, where each request draws down a pool of AI credits instead of a flat monthly fee. If your team relies on Copilot, costs will now track how heavily you actually use it — worth watching before the next budget cycle.

Source: [GitHub Blog ↗](https://github.blog/news-insights/company-news/github-copilot-is-moving-to-usage-based-billing/)

### A Tool That Builds a Dossier on Anyone From a Username

Maigret, now trending on GitHub, takes a single username and hunts for matching accounts across more than 3,000 websites, stitching together a profile of someone's online footprint. It's pitched as a research tool, but it's also a blunt reminder of how much can be reconstructed from the same handle reused everywhere.

Source: [GitHub ↗](https://github.com/soxoj/maigret)

### A Mac Dictation App That Never Leaves Your Computer

FluidVoice is a free, open-source dictation tool for Mac that turns speech into text entirely on your own machine, with nothing sent to a server. For anyone who drafts emails or notes by talking — and would rather their voice not pass through someone else's cloud — it's a private alternative to the built-in options.

Source: [GitHub ↗](https://github.com/altic-dev/FluidVoice)

### LongCat-2.0: A 1.6-Trillion-Parameter Model That Only Wakes Part of Itself

**What happened**
A new open model called LongCat-2.0 has 1.6 trillion total parameters but activates just 48 billion of them for any given word it processes. That design, called a mixture-of-experts, routes each request to a small relevant slice of the network instead of running the whole thing.

**Why it matters**
It's how labs keep models huge on paper while holding running costs down: you pay compute only for the 'experts' a question actually needs. The approach is becoming the default for frontier-scale open models, making trillion-parameter systems practical to serve.

Source: [LongCat ↗](https://longcat.chat/blog/longcat-2.0/)
