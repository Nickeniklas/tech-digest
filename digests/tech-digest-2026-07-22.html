A Court Approves Anthropic's $1.5 Billion Settlement Over Pirated Books

A federal judge approved a $1.5 billion settlement between Anthropic, the company behind the Claude assistant, and a group of authors who said it used pirated copies of their books to train its AI.

DetailValue
Settlement amount$1.5 billion
The claimPirated books used to train Claude
StatusApproved by judge
What this means

This is the clearest price tag yet on training AI with copyrighted work without permission, and a warning to every company building these tools. For writers, lawyers, and publishers, it sets an early benchmark for what unlicensed use of creative work can cost.

▸ OpenAI Opens ChatGPT to Advertisers OpenAI

OpenAI launched a way for businesses to buy ads inside ChatGPT, the chatbot hundreds of millions of people use daily. It's the company's first real step toward placing sponsored content alongside the AI's answers. If you rely on ChatGPT for research or recommendations, expect to start seeing paid placements — and marketers now have a brand-new channel to reach you.

▸ Google Ships Faster, Cheaper Gemini Flash Models Google

Google released Gemini 3.6 Flash alongside two lighter versions: Flash-Lite and a security-focused Flash Cyber. Flash models are the fast, low-cost options built for everyday work like summarizing and drafting, rather than heavy reasoning. ▸ EU's Top Court Rules VPNs Are Lawful Tools TechRadar

In a copyright case, the European Union's highest court ruled that VPNs — software that hides your internet location — are legitimate technical tools and not evidence of wrongdoing on their own. The decision protects a technology millions of people use for privacy and to reach blocked services.

VIA TechRadar ↗

▸ GitHub's Community Passes $100 Million for Open-Source Maintainers GitHub Blog

GitHub said its Sponsors program has now channeled more than $100 million from companies and individuals to the volunteers who build and maintain free, open-source software. Much of the code running the modern internet depends on these often-unpaid maintainers, so the milestone is a rare bit of good news for people who quietly keep the lights on.

Inside the Simulators Teaching Robots to Move

NVIDIA published an overview of how 'physical AI' — robots and self-driving systems — is trained largely in simulation, where a virtual robot can practice walking or grasping millions of times before touching the real world. The piece maps the current tools for building these physics-accurate virtual environments.

Why it matters

Real-world robot data is slow and expensive to collect, so simulation is how most modern robotics progress actually happens. If you follow robotics or automation, this is the plumbing underneath the polished demos.

OpenAI and Hugging Face Detail an AI-Driven Break-In

Previously covered on 2026-07-17: Hugging Face disclosed that attackers, aided by AI acting largely on its own, broke into part of its production systems. Since then, OpenAI and Hugging Face jointly explained that the intrusion surfaced during a model evaluation, and laid out what the automated attacker did and how the teams contained it.

Why it matters

This is one of the first well-documented cases of an AI system driving a real intrusion faster than human attackers would. For security teams, it's a preview of a threat that automates the tedious parts of hacking.
