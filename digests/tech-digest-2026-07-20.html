Lead Story

Leaning on AI for Answers Can Make You More Confident and Less Right

A new study found that people who received AI advice ended up more confident in their answers but actually less accurate. The assistant's fluent, self-assured suggestions tended to crowd out people's own judgment rather than sharpen it.

What this means

If you use AI at work — for drafting, research, or decisions — treat its confident tone as a cue to double-check, not a reason to relax. The danger isn't the wrong answer you catch; it's the wrong answer you feel sure about.

Claude Code Rebuilt on Bun, With Rust Under the Hood

Anthropic's command-line coding tool, Claude Code, now runs on Bun — a faster JavaScript runtime — with performance-critical parts written in Rust, a language prized for speed and tight memory control.

An AI Model Reportedly Cracked a Long-Standing Algebra Puzzle

A mathematician reported that Claude Fable, one of Anthropic's newest models, produced a counterexample to the Jacobian Conjecture — a decades-old open problem in algebra about when a certain kind of polynomial map can be run in reverse.

Why it matters

It's another sign of AI models contributing genuinely new mathematics rather than just reciting known results. As with every such claim, the work still needs careful human checking before the wider math community accepts it.
