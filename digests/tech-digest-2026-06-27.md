## The U.S. Government Is Now Vetting Who Can Use the Most Powerful AI

**What happened**
OpenAI previewed GPT-5.6 "Sol," its next-generation model, and said the U.S. government will vet who is allowed to use it. The same day, the U.S. cleared Anthropic to release its powerful Mythos model only to a list of "trusted" American organisations.

**What this means**
For the first time, access to the strongest commercial AI may depend on who you work for and where you are, not just whether you can pay. If you're at a smaller firm, a school, or outside the U.S., expect a gap to open between the tools the well-connected can use and the ones everyone else gets.

### AI Is Forcing Mathematicians to Rethink What a Proof Is

AI systems are now producing mathematical results so long and dense that humans struggle to fully check them, raising the question of what counts as a proof if no person can read it end to end. The debate matters beyond maths: it's the same trust problem facing anyone who relies on an AI answer they can't fully verify.

Source: [IEEE Spectrum ↗](https://spectrum.ieee.org/ai-in-mathematics)

## Under the Hood

### How Big Is the Gap Between Open and Closed AI Models, Really?

**What happened**
A new analysis weighs "open weights" models — ones you can download and run yourself — against closed models like GPT and Claude that you can only rent through an API. The argument is that open models have closed much of the quality gap for everyday tasks, while the very hardest reasoning and the largest scale still favour the closed labs.

**Why it matters**
If you're a team deciding whether to build on a model you control versus one you rent, this is the core trade-off: ownership, privacy, and predictable cost on one side; peak capability and zero maintenance on the other. The gap narrowing means "run it yourself" is now a serious option for far more use cases than a year ago.

Source: [Doubleword ↗](https://blog.doubleword.ai/frontier-os-llm)

### AWS Lambda Can Now Run Isolated "MicroVMs" on Demand

**What happened**
Amazon added a way to spin up tiny, fully isolated virtual machines — microVMs — through AWS Lambda, with control over their full lifecycle. In plain terms, developers can now create disposable, secure sandboxes that start in a flash and shut down when the work is done.

**Why it matters**
This is aimed squarely at AI agents and untrusted code: when a program writes and runs code on the fly, you want it boxed into a throwaway environment that can't touch anything else. Cheap, fast, isolated sandboxes are becoming basic infrastructure for the agent era.

Source: [AWS ↗](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/aws/run-isolated-sandboxes-with-full-lifecycle-control-aws-lambda-introduces-microvms/)
