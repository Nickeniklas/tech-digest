## Google's Finnish data centre work halted over forest clearing

**What happened**
The Guardian reports that work linked to Google's data centres in Finland has been temporarily halted, with critics saying forest was cut without the required environmental impact assessment.

**What this means**
The buildings that run AI services need land, power and water, and local rules are starting to push back. If your organisation leans on cloud AI, expect more questions about where it runs and what it costs the environment — useful context for anyone writing sustainability reports or procurement policies.

Source: [The Guardian ↗](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/oct/06/google-datacentres-finland-temporary-halt-environmental-impact-assessment)

## Quick Hits

### New York City's "Click to Cancel" rule is now in effect

New York City's rule requiring businesses to make cancelling a subscription as easy as signing up has taken effect. If you run or market a subscription service in the city, check that your cancellation flow is a simple online step, not a phone call or a maze.

Source: [NYC.gov ↗](https://www.nyc.gov/main/click-to-cancel)

### Fingerprints are the new cookies

A widely shared essay argues that browser fingerprinting — identifying you from the unique mix of your device and browser settings — is replacing cookies as the main way websites track you. Unlike cookies, you can't simply clear it, which matters for anyone who relies on privacy controls or works in online advertising.

Source: [royfish.dev ↗](https://royfish.dev/fingerprints-are-the-new-cookies)

### Karpathy: people increasingly disagree about what AI can do

Andrej Karpathy, a well-known AI researcher and OpenAI co-founder, posted that the gap in shared understanding of what AI language models can actually do is widening. If conversations about AI at your workplace feel like people are describing different tools, that is the gap he means.

Source: [X (Andrej Karpathy) ↗](https://twitter.com/karpathy/status/2109361546505966046)

### GitHub: three skills that matter more as AI writes code

GitHub says AI is reshaping developer careers and names three skills to strengthen: directing AI agents, critically reviewing their output, and keeping human judgment at the centre. The same three apply to anyone handing work to AI, not just programmers.

Source: [GitHub Blog ↗](https://github.blog/ai-and-ml/ai-is-rewriting-the-developer-career-ladder-heres-how-to-stand-out/)

## Under the Hood

### A public leaderboard for AI voices in many languages

**What happened**
Hugging Face launched the Open TTS Leaderboard, which scores text-to-speech models (AI that reads text aloud) across multiple languages and on voice cloning — copying a specific person's voice.

**Why it matters**
Most voice AI is judged mainly in English. A shared, open scoreboard makes it easier for teams building audiobooks, accessibility tools or customer service bots in other languages to compare options on equal terms.

Source: [Hugging Face Blog ↗](https://huggingface.co/blog/open-tts-leaderboard)

### Sakana AI: AI systems that improve themselves as a group

**What happened**
Tokyo-based Sakana AI published research on recursive self-improvement through collective intelligence — many AI agents working together to make the overall system better over time, rather than one model improving alone.

**Why it matters**
Self-improving AI is one of the field's most debated ideas. Approaching it through groups of cooperating agents is a different route worth watching for researchers and anyone tracking where AI capability gains will come from.

Source: [Sakana AI ↗](https://pub.sakana.ai/mass/)
