## OpenAI reports three new discoveries in maths, computer science and physics

**What happened**
OpenAI published a post describing three new results in mathematics, computer science and physics, built with academic researchers. One accompanying preprint argues that the Exponential Time Hypothesis — a widely assumed limit on how fast certain hard problems can be solved — is false.

**What this means**
AI is moving from summarising research to contributing to it, but a preprint is not yet a verified result. If you work in research, education or anywhere that relies on expert judgment, expect more 'AI-assisted discovery' claims — and treat peer review as the moment they become real.

Source: [OpenAI ↗](https://openai.com/index/building-scientific-understanding-with-academic-communities/)

### Cloudflare's AI service leaked responses between users

Cloudflare disclosed that its Workers AI service exposed parts of some requests to other users when running the GLM-5.3 model, affecting 155 responses. The cause was a shared short-term memory the model uses while writing answers. It's a reminder that what you type into a hosted AI tool passes through shared infrastructure.

Source: [Cloudflare ↗](https://blog.cloudflare.com/kv-cache-exposure-remediation/)

## Under the Hood

### Blaze claims Rust builds up to 92x faster

**What happened**
A new open-source project, Blaze, says it makes incremental builds of Rust programs — rebuilding after a small code change — up to 92 times faster than Rust's standard tools, cargo and rustc.

**Why it matters**
Slow rebuilds are one of the main complaints about Rust. Faster feedback loops help developers, and also AI coding agents that rebuild code many times while working. Treat 'up to' as a best case until you've tried it on your own project.

Source: [GitHub — indexable-inc/blaze ↗](https://github.com/indexable-inc/blaze)

### Sebastian Raschka's course on building reasoning models from scratch

**What happened**
Machine learning author Sebastian Raschka released a from-scratch video course on building reasoning models — AI models that work through a problem step by step before answering.

**Why it matters**
Reasoning is how most of today's leading models get their gains. Building one yourself is the most direct way to understand what 'thinking' modes in AI products actually do. Aimed at people comfortable with Python.

Source: [Ahead of AI (Sebastian Raschka) ↗](https://magazine.sebastianraschka.com/p/building-reasoning-models)
