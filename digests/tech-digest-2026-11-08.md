## Brain-wave headsets may be collecting your brain data to train AI

**What happened**
A report in Science says makers of consumer brain-wave headsets — wearables sold for meditation, focus or sleep — are harvesting users' brain data to train AI, without telling them.

**What this means**
Brain signals are among the most personal data there is, and most privacy rules weren't written with them in mind. If your workplace or school is considering wellness or focus headsets, it's worth asking exactly what data leaves the device and where it goes.

Source: [Science ↗](https://www.science.org/content/article/wearable-device-makers-are-harvesting-brain-data-train-ai-without-telling-users?referrer=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.google.com%2F)

## Quick Hits

### Claude Code's "Don't Ask Again" option has leaked passwords into 1,122 public files

Security firm Apiiro reports that Claude Code's "Don't Ask Again" permission option has led to passwords and other secrets ending up in 1,122 public files on GitHub. If you or your team use AI coding assistants, check what you've approved permanently — a convenience click can quietly become a security leak.

Source: [Apiiro ↗](https://apiiro.com/blog/coding-agents-leaking-secrets-github-dont-ask-again)

### Your Mac may be quietly sorting people into family members

A widely shared write-up says every Mac ships with an undocumented classifier — a small AI that sorts things into categories — that guesses which people in your life are family, as part of Apple Intelligence's personal knowledge graph. It's a reminder that on-device AI builds a detailed picture of your relationships, even when you never asked it to.

Source: [8rank.ai ↗](https://8rank.ai/game/apple-intelligence-personal-knowledge-graph)

### Apple's 'grey list' of words your iPhone won't help you spell

The Guardian reports that iPhones keep a grey list of words and phrases that autocorrect and suggestions won't help you type — including some perfectly ordinary insults. If you've ever wondered why your phone refuses to complete a word, this is likely why.

Source: [The Guardian ↗](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/nov/06/struggling-to-spell-male-chauvinist-pig-apple-grey-list-means-your-iphone-wont-help-you)

## Under the Hood

### Mistral Large 4 lands on Hugging Face

**What happened**
French AI company Mistral's Large 4 model — nicknamed 'Le Chonk' — is now available on Hugging Face, the main public hub for sharing AI models.

**Why it matters**
Large models from Mistral give developers and companies a European alternative to US providers. Being on Hugging Face means teams can download and test it with the same tools they already use for other open models.

Source: [Hugging Face Blog ↗](https://huggingface.co/blog/mistral-large4)

### MaxxBench asks whether AI coding scores can be trusted

**What happened**
A new benchmark project, MaxxBench, opens with a pointed question about a popular AI coding test: "Nice DeepSWE score. But is it true?" The project explains why it set out to build its own way of checking how well AI models actually solve software tasks.

**Why it matters**
Companies advertise their AI models with benchmark scores, but a test only means something if models can't game it. Independent checks like this help anyone choosing an AI tool tell real ability from good marketing.

Source: [MaxxBench ↗](https://maxxbench.unlap.ai/research/why-maxxbench/)
