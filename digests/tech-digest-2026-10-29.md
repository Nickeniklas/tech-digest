## Anthropic releases Claude Opus 5.5, at 40% lower cost

**What happened**
Anthropic introduced Claude Opus 5.5, the first model in its new Claude 5.5 family. The company says it performs at the level of its top model, Claude Fable 5.1, on most work, leads in coding and knowledge work, and costs 40% less to run than Opus 5 on typical workloads.

**What this means**
If your team uses Claude for drafting, research or analysis, you may get close to top-tier quality at a noticeably lower price. That matters most for organisations — schools, law firms, agencies — that have been holding back on AI because of cost.

## Under the Hood

### NVIDIA's Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe: speech-to-text you can steer

**What happened**
NVIDIA released Nemotron 3.5 Transcribe along with Granary v2, a multilingual speech dataset. The model is designed to be prompted and adapted, so a single system can produce different outputs — a translation, speaker labels, or correctly formatted specialist terms — depending on the job.

**Why it matters**
A plain transcript often isn't enough: meetings need to know who said what, and medical dictation needs jargon spelled right. A promptable, adaptable transcription model lets developers cover those cases without building a separate system for each one.

Source: [Hugging Face Blog ↗](https://huggingface.co/blog/nvidia/nemotron-transcribe-granary)

### Qwenputer: a working computer chip simulated inside an AI model

**What happened**
A developer built Qwenputer, a functioning, deterministic RISC-V CPU — a simple processor design — running inside the Qwen3.5 language model. Deterministic means it gives the same answer every time for the same input, unlike a normal chatbot.

**Why it matters**
It's an experiment rather than a product, but it shows that a language model's internals can be shaped to carry out exact, step-by-step computation, which is relevant to research on making AI more reliable at precise tasks.

Source: [divagr.com ↗](https://divagr.com/blog/qwenputer)
