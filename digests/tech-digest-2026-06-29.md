## Central Bankers Warn the AI Boom Could Trigger a Financial Crash

**What happened**
Senior figures at the world's central banks cautioned that the enormous sums being borrowed and invested to build AI — data centers, chips, and the companies behind them — increasingly resemble the kind of overheated, debt-fueled bet that has ended in crashes before. The concern isn't AI itself, but how much of the financial system is now tied to it paying off.

**What this means**
When regulators start using the word 'crash', it tends to ripple outward — into pensions, savings, hiring, and the stability of the firms you work with. You don't need to own tech stocks to feel a downturn that starts in tech, so it's worth knowing this conversation is now happening at the top.

### A Chinese Open Model Claims It Beats Claude at Finding Security Flaws

Security firm Semgrep says GLM 5.2 — a freely downloadable model from China — outperformed Anthropic's Claude on its own cybersecurity benchmarks. It's another sign that open models you can run yourself are closing the gap with the expensive, locked-down ones. For anyone weighing AI vendors, the 'you must pay a US company' assumption is weakening fast.

### A Brown Professor Says Most of a Class Cheated With AI

A professor at Brown University publicly accused a large share of students of using AI to cheat on an exam, reigniting the debate over how schools handle tools that can write a passable essay in seconds. ### Researchers Build a Pixel That Can Both Show and See Light

Engineers at ETH Zurich created a new kind of pixel that can display an image and detect light at the same time. In plain terms, the same surface could one day be both a screen and a camera — handy for things like phones or laptops that no longer need a separate notch or hole for the front camera.

## Under the Hood

### 66 Years of Falling Memory Prices, in One Chart

**What happened**
Stanford published a historical dataset tracking the price of computer memory from 1960 to 2026. The trend is staggering: memory that once cost billions of dollars per gigabyte now costs a couple of dollars, a decline of roughly a billion-fold over a single human lifetime.

**Why it matters**
Almost everything in modern computing — smartphones, cloud storage, and especially the giant AI models in today's news — only exists because memory got absurdly cheap. This chart is the quiet backstory to why AI suddenly became possible: the hardware finally got cheap enough to throw mountains of data at it.

Source: [Stanford ↗](https://dam.stanford.edu/memory-prices.html)

### There's a New Fastest Supercomputer in the World

**What happened**
At the ISC 2026 conference, the TOP500 list — the official ranking of the world's most powerful supercomputers — crowned a new number one. These rankings measure raw calculating speed, and a change at the top usually reflects a country or lab bringing a major new machine online.

**Why it matters**
Supercomputers are where weather forecasting, drug discovery, climate modeling, and frontier AI training actually happen. Who holds the top spot is partly a scientific milestone and partly a geopolitical scoreboard — a signal of which nations are investing hardest in the compute that underpins modern research.
