A New York lawmaker wants the video games you buy to actually belong to you — and keep working after the publisher moves on.

Lead Story

New York bill would make bought games yours to keep

A New York state representative has announced a game ownership bill that would require publishers to sell games to consumers outright, rather than selling a licence to play them. Publishers would also have to keep a game working after official support ends.

What this means

Today, much of what you 'buy' digitally — games, films, e-books — is a licence that can be withdrawn or stop working. The bill is only a proposal for now, but if it passes it could set an example for other digital purchases; lawyers and anyone advising on consumer rights will want to watch it.

Deep Dive

Lean now ships with verified kernels

Lean, a programming language used to write mathematical proofs that a computer can check, now ships with verified kernels, according to its creator Leonardo de Moura. The kernel is the small core of Lean that has the final say on whether a proof is valid.

Why it matters

Lean is increasingly used to check proofs produced by AI models, so everything rests on its kernel being correct. Verifying that core itself strengthens the guarantee that a proof Lean accepts really holds — useful for mathematicians and for anyone relying on AI-generated reasoning.
